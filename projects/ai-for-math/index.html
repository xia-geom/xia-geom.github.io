Research results

Claude-designed scorpion neurotoxin binders undergo laboratory tests

Published 2026-10-20 · Anthropic with Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience · Permalink

Anthropic reports laboratory binding tests of 211 protein designs, of which 17 met a predefined binding criterion for individual scorpion neurotoxins. The study does not establish neutralization of venom or clinical efficacy.

Source note: Company-led experimental binding measurements, not an antivenom; designs and data released.

  • Read source: Anthropic study and data links

Laboratory feedback improves AI-designed aptamer binders

Published 2026-10-17 · Zachary Cutts and collaborators · Permalink

Researchers test AI-guided aptamer designs for thrombin and myoglobin over three design-test-revision cycles. They report improved target-binding hit rates in later rounds and use laboratory measurements to expose off-target binding and assay artifacts.

Source note: Preprint with wet-lab binding measurements; no demonstration of therapeutic efficacy.

  • Read source: Original preprint

Evaluator-guided language models optimize quantum circuits

Published 2026-10-15 · Andi Gu, Alek H. Kemeny, Susanne F. Yelin and Mikhail D. Lukin · Permalink

A preprint couples LLM-generated reversible quantum circuits to an independent correctness and cost evaluator. The authors report lower Toffoli-gate and qubit requirements in seven of eight tested computational tasks, and a new modular-inversion architecture in the eighth.

Source note: Preprint with evaluator-checked algorithms, not a reported fault-tolerant quantum hardware experiment.

  • Read source: Original preprint

Machine learning improves subseasonal weather forecast calibration

Published 2026-10-14 · Hannah Guan, Soukayna Mouatadid and collaborators · Permalink

A Nature study applies probabilistic machine-learning bias correction to existing two-to-six-week weather forecasts. The authors report improvements over tested baseline forecasts for a majority of temperature, pressure and precipitation targets.

Source note: Peer-reviewed modeling and forecast evaluation; gains depend on the baselines and regions tested.

  • Read source: Nature research article

OpenAI publishes AI-generated mathematics with subsequent corrections

Published 2026-10-06 · OpenAI · Permalink

OpenAI released a large collection of AI-produced manuscripts and proof artifacts. The current repository lists 719 manuscripts across 372 families, with about 42% of headline results formalized in Lean. An October 7 update records three withdrawals caused by a sign error and revisions to 14 others.

Source note: Many claims remain unformalized; source authors themselves warn of possible gaps. This page has not audited the proofs.

  • Read source: OpenAI announcement
  • Read source: Release repository
  • Read source: Corrections and withdrawals

Analytic 3D plasma equilibria without stellarator symmetry

Published 2026-10-05 · Opal Issan, Jonathan Citrin, Matt Landreman and Brendan Tracey · Permalink

Extending Landreman's recent AI-assisted analytic counterexamples to a formulation of Grad's conjecture, the authors introduce symmetry-breaking parameters that preserve exact non-axisymmetric magnetohydrodynamic equilibria.

Source note: Mathematical preprint and analytic benchmark for codes; not evidence of improved fusion confinement.

  • Read source: Follow-up preprint
  • Read source: Earlier AI-assisted construction

AI-assisted search discovers magic-state distillation protocols

Published 2026-10-05 · Shubham P. Jain, Adam Wills and Shraddha Singh · Permalink

The authors combine symmetry, language-model search, deterministic solving and independent checking to identify 699 classes of quantum magic-state factories, including 564 new ones, with improved overhead in specified regimes.

Source note: Preprint with computationally checked constructions; not a hardware demonstration.

  • Read source: Original preprint

Claude computes a nine-loop amplitude in planar N=4 SYM

Published 2026-09-25 · Matt von Hippel, with Lance Dixon · Permalink

Anthropic physicists Liam Fitzpatrick and Siddharth Mishra-Sharma used Claude to compute the six-particle MHV amplitude in planar N=4 super Yang–Mills theory at nine loops by two established routes. The source presents this theory as a toy model used to develop amplitude methods.

Source note: Anthropic-hosted guest report with released result files; Lance Dixon reports independent validation of the nine-loop result. The released function-level result notes that it has no second independent computation.

  • Read source: Anthropic / Matt von Hippel & Lance Dixon
  • Read source: Cosmic9 result files

Claude identifies a previously uncharacterized enzyme system

Published 2026-09-23 · Anthropic life sciences team · Permalink

Anthropic reports that agents screening microbial DNA found an array-associated reverse transcriptase (ART) system. Preliminary laboratory work detected small RNA products, but its biological function remains unknown.

Source note: Company research report; not a validated gene-editing technology.

  • Read source: Anthropic research report

Reported finite-time blow-up for forced Navier–Stokes

Published 2026-09-08 · OpenAI · Permalink

OpenAI reports an AI-produced analytical proof and Lean formalization of finite-time blow-up from rest under smooth external forcing, with finite energy. The stated scope is the forced alternatives C and D, not unforced Navier–Stokes regularity.

Source note: Author-reported result with released artifacts; no independent proof audit by this page.

Tao invites mathematicians to use AI to derive explicit bounds for a hypothetical tenth class-number-one discriminant from recent arguments on Siegel zeroes, highlighting checked contributions and explanatory value.

IAS-hosted advisory group on AI and mathematics

Published 2026-09-21 · Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence (AGMAI) · Permalink

An independent group of mathematicians hosted at the Institute for Advanced Study says it will advise AI companies on their interactions with mathematical research, including responsible presentation and release of results. Its current task is advising OpenAI on how to coordinate results that OpenAI reports were produced by an internal model.

Source note: Institutional/group announcement; AGMAI is independent of AI companies. Its reference to OpenAI’s reported results is not a validation of those mathematical claims.

A declaration on AI and the aims of mathematics

Published 2026-09-11 · Math and AI (Terence Tao et al.) · Permalink

The signatories argue that mathematical progress should be evaluated through understanding and the transmission of ideas, not only solved problems. They raise concerns about attribution, exposition and the training of future mathematicians.

Source note: Collective viewpoint; not a unanimous position of all mathematicians.
