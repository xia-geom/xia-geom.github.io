Six themes for a working group

Open a theme for questions, readings and a session idea. These are starting points for discussion, not a fixed programme or a collective position.

AI and mathematical research Research practice, proof assistants, access and publication.

Questions to discuss

  • Which parts of a mathematician’s work are well suited to AI, and how should its contribution be checked?
  • What can Lean, Isabelle and Coq check? How do specialized AI agents fit into a research workflow?
  • Who has access to models and computing resources? How should copyright and confidential research data be protected?
  • How should research funding, attribution and publication evolve as more mathematical work is automated?

Readings and resources

  • Complex structure on the six-sphere — shared manuscript

    Proposed construction in a public PDF; not independently verified here. The author attribution and AI contribution require confirmation.

  • Fermat’s Last Theorem — Anthropic’s formalization report

    Read as a report about formalizing an existing theorem, not its first mathematical proof.

  • Forced Navier–Stokes — OpenAI’s announcement and proof links

    The announced result uses smooth external forcing. Separate the stated theorem, released artifacts and independent verification.

  • Terence Tao — interview on AI and mathematics (video)

    A researcher’s perspective; not an institutional statement.

  • Un résultat sans chemin — RogueESR

    A critical commentary to discuss alongside the original research announcement.

Session idea: Choose one reported result and separate its exact statement, the role of AI, the available evidence and the checks still needed.

Education and early-career mathematicians Undergraduate programmes, doctoral training and mathematical skills.

Questions to discuss

  • How can we continue to attract students, and how should we explain the value of studying mathematics?
  • Should undergraduate programmes and assessment change? Which skills should students develop without delegating them to AI?
  • What might a mathematics PhD profile look like in 2027 or 2035? Which skills become more important, and what knowledge could be lost?

Readings and resources

  • PhD education summit — Bryna Kra and Rachel Ward

    Draft recommendations hosted on Tao’s blog; a discussion document, not adopted UQAM policy.

  • INTER-MATH-AI training programme

    An example of interdisciplinary mathematics and AI training, with ethics and applications.

Session idea: Take one course or doctoral activity and discuss what should be learned, what could be delegated and how understanding would be assessed.

UQAM and the mathematical community Public statements, collective choices and research networks.

Questions to discuss

  • How should the department connect with existing initiatives, and what role could the CRM play?
  • Should the department adopt shared rules or a public position on AI? Where do the available statements agree or differ?
  • Could an open mathematics AI be hosted at a university, with transparent costs and environmental impacts?
  • What could a Québec research initiative on the mathematics of AI contribute, and how would it be funded?

Readings and resources

  • Math and AI declaration

    A collective viewpoint on understanding, attribution and the aims of mathematics.

  • Leiden Declaration on AI and Mathematics

    Community recommendations for the practice of mathematics; compare the text with other positions.

  • SMF board — Navier–Stokes and OpenAI

    An institutional position on the announcements and their ethical implications, not a proof audit.

  • Association for Human Mathematics

    The association’s own presentation and position.

  • Proofs and Prompts

    Community essays and discussions; attribute each contribution to its author.

Institutes and networks to explore

  • McGill — McCAIS

  • Université Laval — Institut intelligence et données
  • ICARM — Institute for Computer-Aided Reasoning in Mathematics

Session idea: Compare two public statements and identify concrete questions for the department. The linked initiatives are resources to explore, not partners of this page.

AI for mathematics and the environment Computing infrastructure, energy use and the limits of footprint estimates.

Questions to discuss

  • How can we estimate the environmental impact of a mathematical task assisted by AI?
  • What can data-centre maps and footprint calculators tell us, and which assumptions or missing data prevent a reliable comparison?

Readings and resources

  • EcoLogits Calculator

    A tool for estimating impacts of model inference; estimates are not direct measurements of a particular research project.

  • EcoLogits — methodology and documentation

    Read the assumptions and scope alongside the calculator.

  • Data Centre Map — Montréal

    Infrastructure directory, not a measurement of energy consumed by AI for mathematics.

  • Data Centre Map — Paris

    A second geographic reference for the infrastructure discussion.

Session idea: Estimate one clearly specified use case and list the assumptions and missing information before drawing conclusions.

Epistemology and AI safety Proof, understanding, the limits of language models and AI safety.

Questions to discuss

  • How can we understand mathematics found by machines? Could we know that a statement is true without understanding why?
  • What does doing mathematics mean when exploration is largely automated?
  • Which claims about general intelligence, model limitations and existential risks are established, conjectural or matters of disagreement?

Readings and resources

  • Artificial Intelligence and the Structure of Mathematics

    Essay by Maissam Barkeshli, Michael R. Douglas and Michael H. Freedman on the structure of proofs and automated discovery.

  • LawZero — AI safety research

    The initiative’s own account of its research direction; not a settled conclusion about AI risks.

Session idea: Discuss a concrete distinction between producing a proof, checking it and explaining it to another mathematician.

Understanding how AI works Neural networks, learning theory and the mathematics of language models.

Questions to discuss

  • How do neural networks and large language models work, and which mathematical ideas explain their basic operations?
  • Which foundational texts help distinguish a working mechanism from a theorem about learning or generalization?

Readings and resources

  • 3Blue1Brown — neural networks

    Visual explanations as an entry point.

  • Deep Learning — Goodfellow, Bengio and Courville

    Textbook: mathematical background and neural-network methods.

  • Mathematics for Machine Learning

    Deisenroth, Faisal and Ong: linear algebra, probability, optimization and learning.

  • Understanding Machine Learning: From Theory to Algorithms

    Shai Shalev-Shwartz and Shai Ben-David: the authors’ page for the learning-theory textbook.

  • The Simple Mathematics of Large Language Models

    Joseph L. Breeden’s introductory working paper (PDF).

Session idea: Work through a small example and explain which quantities are defined by the architecture and which are learned from data.

Reported finite-time blow-up for forced Navier–Stokes

Published 2026-09-08 · OpenAI · Permalink

OpenAI reports an AI-produced analytical proof and Lean formalization of finite-time blow-up from rest under smooth external forcing, with finite energy. The stated scope is the forced alternatives C and D, not unforced Navier–Stokes regularity.

Source note: Author-reported result with released artifacts; no independent proof audit by this page.

Published 2026-09-24 · International Council for Industrial and Applied Mathematics (ICIAM) · Permalink

ICIAM welcomes AI-assisted discovery while emphasizing reproducibility, attribution, validation and human judgement. It also calls for reporting unsuccessful approaches and maintaining mathematical expertise.

Source note: Institutional statement; distinct from individual opinions.

A declaration on AI and the aims of mathematics

Published 2026-09-11 · Math and AI (Terence Tao et al.) · Permalink

The signatories argue that mathematical progress should be evaluated through understanding and the transmission of ideas, not only solved problems. They raise concerns about attribution, exposition and the training of future mathematicians.

Source note: Collective viewpoint; not a unanimous position of all mathematicians.

About the reading guide and news

The six themes are a lasting reading guide, maintained separately from the news. Questions and session ideas are proposals for discussion, not departmental policy. Linked institutions are resources to explore, not partners of this page.

News notes are prepared with an AI research assistant. The date above records the last completed check of news sources, not a site rebuild or a guarantee of daily updates. Coverage is selective.

Source checking is not independent proof verification. Reported results, formalizations of known theorems, competition outcomes and opinions are identified separately. Following a link does not imply endorsement of its claims.
